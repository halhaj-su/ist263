Common Car Problems and Warning Signs

Cars usually give you some kind of warning when something is wrong. The warning might be a dashboard light, a strange sound, a smell, shaking, or a change in the way the car drives.

Learning to recognize these signs can help you deal with a problem before it becomes worse.

Dashboard Warning Lights

Modern cars have warning lights that tell the driver when the vehicle detects a problem.

Check Engine Light

This light can appear for many different reasons. Sometimes the problem is small, but other times it can be more serious. If the light stays on, the vehicle should be checked. If it is flashing, have the problem inspected promptly.

Battery Light

This can mean there is a problem with the vehicle's charging system. The battery, alternator, wiring, or another electrical component may need to be checked.

Oil Warning Light

An oil pressure warning can mean your engine has low oil pressure. Stop safely and shut off the engine; check your owner's manual for instructions.

Temperature Warning Light

This usually means the engine is becoming too hot. Stop in a safe place and turn the engine off.

Other Signs You Should Notice

Strange Noises

You know what your car normally sounds like, so a new noise can be an important warning sign.

  • Squeaking when braking
  • Grinding noises
  • Clicking when trying to start
  • Knocking from the engine
  • Humming or vibration while driving
  • Rattling underneath the vehicle

A strange sound does not automatically tell you exactly what is broken, but it is a sign that you should investigate the problem.

Overheating

Possible signs include a high temperature gauge, a temperature warning light, steam coming from under the hood, or a strong smell.

If your vehicle is overheating, continuing to drive it can cause more damage. Find a safe place to stop and allow the vehicle to cool down. Never open a hot radiator or coolant system because the coolant can be under pressure and cause serious burns.

Dead or Weak Battery

A dead battery is one of the most common reasons a car will not start. Clicking when trying to start or dim lights may be signs of a weak battery.

A jump-start may get the vehicle running if the battery is discharged, but if the problem keeps happening, the battery or charging system should be tested.

What Should I Do?

When Can I Check the Problem Myself?

Some basic things you may be able to check yourself include:

  • Tire pressure
  • Engine oil level
  • Windshield washer fluid
  • Battery terminals
  • Visible tire damage
  • Basic dashboard warning lights

Only work on your vehicle if you understand what you are doing and can do it safely.

When Should I Go to a Mechanic?

Some problems are better handled by a professional. Get help when:

  • The car is overheating
  • The brakes are not working correctly
  • There is a serious fluid leak
  • The engine is making loud or unusual noises
  • A warning light keeps returning
  • The car shakes badly while driving
  • The car repeatedly will not start
  • You are not sure how to repair the problem safely

Part of taking care of your car is knowing what you can safely handle yourself and when the problem needs someone with more experience.
